Two worlds to invade.

The island runs on cherry-blossom light and warm soil. The cave is bioluminescent, underground, and defended by a different roster entirely.

The Island

Cherry blossoms, warm light, and a floating scrap of earth held together by roots. Bush and tree block the path; ladybug and bee are the ones with actual range and bite.

The Cave In development

Bioluminescent and underground. A different roster — bat, firefly, scorpion, spider — and a different tile set. Map layout and defender balance are being iterated now.

You control the swarm.

Draw a path on an 8×8 isometric grid, spend your branch budget spawning creatures onto it, and march them to the Tree of Life. Defenders sit on the board and attack anything that passes through their range. Your job is to get enough creatures through to drain the tree to zero.

The wave clock gives you 60 seconds. Run out of time, or run out of both creatures and branches mid-wave, and you lose. The budget changes each round, the board stays the same, and the path that worked last wave may run straight through a newly-placed tower this one.

The shortest path is bait. It runs straight through defender range zones. A ladybug covers a 1-tile radius; a bee covers 2. You can draw a longer route that threads between them — but that costs more time and more creatures to fill it. Or you can spend up: a snail costs 20 branches and absorbs four hits, a worm costs 1 and dies in one. Neither is always right. The answer changes every wave.

Each biome has its own Tree of Life. Three health thresholds per tree — the draw loop swaps the sprite each frame based on current HP.

Every wave, a budget.

Branches — the in-game currency — carry over wave to wave and scale each round, from 10 on wave 1 to 190 on wave 19. Creatures unlock progressively, so early rounds limit you to the Worm while heavier options come later. Spend on volume now, or hold for something that hits harder.

The panel on the right is the real in-game shop.

Every run fills in the book.

Two biomes, two codex books. Each entry fills in as you encounter the creature — plants, defenders, oddities. One book per world.

Behind the script

Built in Python with Raylib (pyray bindings), pixel art by Angelina Tan. Five creature types, 20 waves on the island, cave biome in progress. A few notes from actually building it:

On Pathfinding

Any path you draw has to prove itself

A BFS resolves the route through whatever shape you lay down, and rejects anything that doesn't connect spawn to goal — before a single creature commits to walking it.

On Bugs

The clouds that vanished and came back

Removing an entity from a list while iterating that same list skips whatever slides into its old index — for exactly one frame. Long enough to see, hard enough to notice why.

On Movement

Linear interpolation, written by hand

Creatures glide between grid cells using lerp — position updates each frame as a fraction of the remaining distance. Every edge case (path end, speed variation, frame rate) required its own fix.

On Rendering

Back-to-front draw order along diagonals

Isometric depth requires rendering back tiles before front ones or sprites overlap incorrectly. The draw loop starts at the bottom left corner and sweeps diagonally — every tile, creature, and tower uses the same coordinate formula to show depth.

On Tile Art

Any path shape resolves to the right sprite automatically

Each tile checks its four neighbors (right, down, left, up) and maps the binary tuple to a texture in a pre-built lookup table. Corner, T-junction, dead end — the tile switches without any manual selection.
